department: Physics Department, School of Science

abstract: >-
  Using density functional theory (DFT) in the open-source CP2K package, I searched for the
  lowest-energy states of bioactive molecules by optimising their geometry. The electronic
  structure was computed with CP2K's Quickstep method (mixed Gaussian and plane-wave, GPW),
  GTH pseudopotentials and the PBE exchange–correlation functional, with geometry and
  electronic structure optimised together. Geometry optimisations were run for paracetamol,
  sildenafil, cytosine, cyclohexanedione, ethyl carbamate, oxalic acid and formamide. For
  each system the grid cutoffs were chosen by comparing run times across settings, and the
  convergence of every self-consistent-field (SCF) run was checked to confirm that the
  simulation reached a more stable, lower-energy state.
methods:
  - Density functional theory (DFT)
  - Geometry optimisation
  - Gaussian and plane-wave method (GPW)
  - Self-consistent field (SCF) with orbital transformation (OT)
  - PBE exchange–correlation functional
  - GTH pseudopotentials
tech:
  - CP2K
highlights:
  - Built the CP2K input for each molecule — basis sets, GTH pseudopotentials, system coordinates and the GLOBAL, FORCE_EVAL and MOTION sections.
  - Chose the CUTOFF and REL_CUTOFF grid settings from single-point runs by comparing run times.
  - Tuned the number of SCF iterations (MAX_SCF) by tracking minimum and mean energy and convergence after every SCF run.
  - Diagnosed runs that failed to converge or converged slowly (method for the electron potentials, orbital transformation, geometry optimiser) and fixed them by testing each change.
